Winter Solstice Night Walk at Walden Ponds: Boulder's Longest Night, on Foot

On Sunday, December 20, 2026, the longest night of the year starts at Walden Ponds Wildlife Habitat with a one-mile walk around the ponds, led by volunteer naturalists. The walk runs from 4:30 to 6:30 pm, from the last of the daylight into full dark, and the open space is yours after hours.

Every fact below comes from Boulder County Parks & Open Space's own event page and registration page, checked November 24, 2026.

The Facts As They Stand

  • Date and time: Sunday, December 20, 2026, 4:30 to 6:30 pm.
  • Place: Walden Ponds Wildlife Habitat, 3893 N. 75th St., Boulder, CO 80301.
  • The walk: A flat, easy, one-mile walk around the ponds, led by volunteer naturalists, with a look at local nocturnal wildlife and the winter constellations.
  • Registration: Required. The registration page lists a minimum of 5 and a maximum of 35 participants.
  • Last year: The 2025 walk, held Sunday, December 21, 2025 at the same venue and hours, reached full capacity and closed registration.
  • Official pages: bouldercounty.gov, Winter Solstice Night Walk at Walden Ponds, and the registration page.

Event facts from Boulder County Parks & Open Space, checked November 24, 2026. The 2025 capacity note comes from the organizer's 2025 event listing at bouldercounty.gov/event/winter-solstice-night-walk-boulder/.

What the Night Is For

The organizer's description is short and plain: experience what the night has to offer. In practice that means a walk around ponds that look different once the sun is down, a chance to notice the animals that keep to the dark, and a sky that the winter constellations turn into a map if you stop long enough to read it. Stand still for a minute near the water and you will hear how quiet a cold evening can get. That is most of the reason to go.

The walk is flat and easy, which makes it a good first night walk for anyone who has only ever done the summer version of Boulder's open space.

Planning a Group Around It

A group of twelve starts with the registration page, not the car. Registration is capped at 35 participants, and last year's walk reached capacity, so the person organizing the trip should register the group early and not wait for the week of the solstice. Once the group is on the list, the evening is simple: leave the canyon with time to spare, arrive before 4:30 pm, and let the volunteer naturalists lead the walk.

For two people or a family, the walk asks for very little. Bring a warm layer, a headlamp or flashlight for the way back, and a snack for afterward. The mile is flat, which is a mercy for younger legs and for anyone who would rather look up at the sky than down at the path.

Pairing It With the December Calendar

If the solstice is one of several December nights on your calendar, the Lights of December Parade comes eight days earlier, on Saturday, December 12, with its 6 pm start downtown. Our preview of that evening covers the route and the reviewing stand, so the two nights make a pair: one lit by floats and marching bands, the other by the sky.

If the Solstice Is the Reason for the Trip

When the walk is the reason for the weekend, plan the rest of the day around it and keep it slow. A late breakfast, a warm afternoon by the fire, and then a drive down the valley with time in hand. The longest night is a good one to be unhurried for.
