Where to Stay for the COLDERBolder 5K

If you are the one holding a running crew together for the COLDERBolder 5K, this page answers one question plainly: where does your group sleep on the first Saturday of December, when everyone has to be standing on the CU campus with a race number in hand before seven in the morning. Sunshine Canyon Retreat is a single private house above Boulder that sleeps up to 14 in five bedrooms, about a twenty minute drive from the race, booked direct with us.

The 2026 race is Saturday, December 5, on the CU Boulder campus, and it is sold out. Everything on this page about the race comes from the organizer's own site at bolderboulder.com; everything about the house comes from our own listing. It is all checked and dated below.

The Morning You Are Booking Around

  • The race: The COLDERBolder 5K, the December sister race to the BOLDERBoulder, created in 2003 and run on the University of Colorado campus.
  • Date and starts: Saturday, December 5, 2026. Race 1 goes off at 7:05 AM and the starts roll through the morning, with the Open Race at 8:55 AM and the Walking Wave at 9:15 AM, per the official FAQs: bolderboulder.com/colder-faqs-2026.
  • The finish: Inside the university's field house, where the organizer promises festivities and holiday spirit, and warmth, which matters in December.
  • Registration: The 2026 race is sold out and registration is closed. There is no race-day registration.
  • Packets: Race-day pickup opens at 6:00 AM in the Balch Fieldhouse. Anyone who chose a partner location, like the Runners Roost on South Broadway in Boulder, has to collect theirs by Friday, December 4.

Why a Sold-Out December Race Is Still the Hard One to Book

The organizer says this race now sells out year after year, so if you are reading this, your crew is probably already in. That makes lodging the last open question. A 7:05 first wave means alarms at five and coffee in the dark, and it means nobody wants to be navigating hotel corridors to find the person holding the safety pins. One house instead of a block of rooms gives you one address for the group chat, one kitchen for the oatmeal and banana station, and one living room where fourteen pairs of running shoes get laid out in a row the night before.

The other truth of this weekend: it is December in Boulder, which is its own kind of beautiful and its own kind of cold. The organizer's motto is Warmth is for Whiners, and the canyon at dawn that Saturday will make a runner out of anyone who repeats it.

The House: One Booking for Up to Fourteen

Sunshine Canyon Retreat sits in the canyon above Boulder, a private mountain home with the town lights below and the dark of the foothills behind it. The details that matter to a race crew:

  • Sleeps up to 14 guests in five bedrooms with four bathrooms, so the 5 a.m. queue for the mirror stays short.
  • Approximately 4,100 square feet, a full kitchen with modern appliances, a living room with a fireplace, and high speed WiFi throughout.
  • A hot tub with mountain views and private parking at the house, which is the real luxury on a morning when campus lots fill early.

Race Morning From the Canyon

The drive down to the CU campus runs about twenty minutes, and at that hour it is a good one: headlights sweeping the canyon switchbacks, the first light going pink on the Divide, the town still glittering below when you come down out of the dark. Race-day parking is in CU lots 391, 169 and 436, paid in the lot that morning, and the organizer also runs a runner drop-off on Stadium Drive next to the buffalo statue, with signage and volunteers to point the way. Bag check is inside the Balch Fieldhouse, and so are the restrooms; there are no porta-potties on the course, so plan the last water stop accordingly.

One honest note on getting around: rideshares can reach the house, but pickups up the canyon are less reliable than drop-offs, especially early in the morning. The plan that never fails a race crew is the group's own car or a rental, driven by whoever drew the late wave.

After the Finish

Walk out of the field house with your medal and your cocoa, and the rest of the day is yours. The smart move from our canyon is the unhurried one: head back up the hill, put the finishers in the hot tub, reheat a real breakfast, and let the campus traffic drain. By early afternoon Pearl Street is fifteen minutes away, all lit for the season, and a December walk on the mall with a roasted coffee in hand is the correct way to end a race day.
