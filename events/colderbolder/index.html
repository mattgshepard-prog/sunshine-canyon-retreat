The COLDERBolder 5K: Boulder's December Race on the CU Campus

Each December, runners in tights and Santa hats take over the University of Colorado campus for the COLDERBolder 5K, the cold-weather sister of the BOLDERBoulder. This page is the evergreen version: what the race is, how its unusual format works, and what a group should plan around, updated each season.

This season's page, with the December 5, 2026 details, start times, and packet pickup, is our 2026 COLDERBolder race brief. For the lodging side, we also wrote where to stay for the COLDERBolder 5K.

What the Race Is

BOLDERBoulder, bolderboulder.com

The COLDERBolder 5K started in 2003 as part of the BOLDERBoulder's racing series, and the organizer now describes it as the sister race to the Memorial Day 10K, one that has grown popular enough to sell out year after year. It is locally owned and operated, run on a fast course through the CU Boulder campus, and it finishes inside the university's field house, where the festivities and the holiday spirit take the chill off. The organizer's own pitch for the morning is fitness, the cold, and the holidays, all at once.

Source: About the COLDERBolder, BOLDERBoulder

How the Format Works, and Why It Matters for a Group

This is the part that makes the COLDERBolder different from every other December 5K. It is not one race. It is fourteen individual races plus a Walking Wave, and the first thirteen put runners who are about the same speed together, sorted by previous race times. The organizer's example: if you finished the BOLDERBoulder in 52 minutes, you line up against people who finished between 52 and 54. Everyone in your race has a genuine shot at winning it, and the top three in each race take home Cold Medals.

The fourteenth is the Open Race, with no qualifying standards, for anyone who wants to run it fast or slow and enjoy the winter morning. After that comes one Walking Wave, open to anyone who would rather take a festive stroll through campus. That wave is the answer for the non-runners in your crew: they still get the course and the field house finish, without a start gun full of expectations.

The trade for a group is that pace-seeded waves split people up. Unless your runners all land in the same wave, they will not start together, and the organizer's own answer for friends and family is the Open Race, which puts everyone on the same start line regardless of speed.

Source: About the COLDERBolder, BOLDERBoulder; wave and group guidance from the COLDERBolder 2026 FAQs

Plan for It to Sell Out

The single most useful thing to know about this race, and the reason to decide early: it sells out. The organizer says so plainly, and the 2026 edition was sold out with registration closed well before race day, with no race-day registration offered. If your group wants in, watch the organizer's registration page when it opens for the next running and commit as a crew. If you miss it, the organizer has offered a virtual option in recent years, with the details for each season on the FAQ page, and the race makes a fine thing to spectate from the field house anyway.

Source: About the COLDERBolder; 2026 registration status from the COLDERBolder 2026 FAQs

The Rules That Trip Groups Up

The course rules are firm, and they are the ones a group planner gets burned by. From the organizer's race rules:

  • Pets stay at home. No dogs on the course, so the family dog waits at the house.
  • No strollers or baby joggers on the course. This is not the race for running it while pushing a kid, unlike some of Boulder's more relaxed fun runs.
  • No skateboards or skates, and no costumes that extend beyond the body. A Santa hat is fine; a full sleigh is not.
  • Bibs are personal. You run in your assigned race, and there is no transferring a bib to a faster cousin.
  • No refunds, exchanges, or transfers on registration.

Results are posted on the organizer's website after the race, and every finisher receives a results certificate by email once the results are finalized.

Source: The COLDERBolder 2026 FAQs; rules are restated by the organizer each season, so check the FAQ page for the year you are running

Race Morning, in Brief

The starts are early and they roll: in 2026, the first seeded race went off at 7:05 in the morning and the Walking Wave at 9:15, with packet pickup at partner locations ending the day before and race-day pickup opening at 6:00 AM in the Balch Fieldhouse near the start. The day's shape is an alarm in the dark, breath hanging in the air on campus, and a warm field house with the festivities by mid-morning. Exact start times, pickup locations, and parking details change a little each year, and we keep them current on the season's page rather than here.

Source: The COLDERBolder 2026 FAQs

Where to Stay for Race Weekend

The race starts on the CU campus and the first waves go off early in the morning, so the lodging question is really a logistics question: where can a whole running crew sleep, eat an early dinner the night before, and still make a morning start without three cars and a spreadsheet. Sunshine Canyon Retreat sleeps up to fourteen about a twenty minute drive down the canyon from the campus, with a hot tub for post-race legs and a kitchen big enough for the pasta the night before. December in the canyon is the quiet season: gold light on the snow, the whole town lit for the holidays below you.
