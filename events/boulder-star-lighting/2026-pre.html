The Boulder Star Lights Up for Its 78th Year

On Veterans Day, the floodlights blink out on the mountainside west of town, and a few hundred bulbs in the shape of a star blink on. Boulder has watched this happen every November since 1947. Here is how the 2026 lighting works, checked against the Chamber and the City, so you and your group can plan the evening.

Skip ahead if you only need the facts: the 2026 ceremony is a livestream on the Boulder Chamber's YouTube channel, Wednesday, November 11, from 6:00 to 6:45 pm Mountain time. But the reason it is worth knowing about is older than almost anyone reading this.

What the Ceremony Is

Boulder Chamber

The Boulder Chamber runs the lighting, and this year the ceremony is a livestream rather than a gathering on the mountain. Held on Veterans Day, the evening honors active duty military and veterans, and it features a Joint Service Color Guard in partnership with the CU Boulder Veterans Alumni Association. The Chamber streams it on its YouTube channel, so anyone, anywhere, can watch the moment the star comes on. The star was first lit in December 1947, the Chamber says, and it has stood through the years as a marker of encouragement and resilience for the town below.

Source: LIVESTREAM: 2026 Boulder Star Lighting Ceremony, Boulder Chamber

Where to Actually See the Star

City of Boulder Open Space and Mountain Parks

One thing has changed from the old years, and the City is plain about it: the star area on Flagstaff Mountain is closed. No hiking up to stand under the bulbs. The City asks everyone to view the star from afar, to protect the wildlife areas nearby and the ground around it, and its open space staff co-light the star with the Chamber on Veterans Day. What the City gives you instead is a long list of places where the star reads beautifully from a distance: Chautauqua Park, Flagstaff Trail, Sawhill Ponds, North Boulder Park, East Boulder Community Park, and open space spots farther out like Marshall Mesa, Coalton, Greenbelt Plateau, Dry Creek, Cherryvale Trailhead, and South Boulder Creek Trail. Pick a place with a clear sightline to the western ridge and the star sits above town like a bookmark in the sky.

Once it is lit, it stays lit. The City says the star shines each evening through the end of the year. Its engineers also fitted it with a remote sensor that turns it on and off at the right time and shuts it off automatically in high winds to prevent fire danger, so a dark star on a windy night is not a malfunction. It is the mountain looking after itself.

Source: View the Boulder Star from Afar, City of Boulder

The short version. Wednesday, November 11, 2026, Veterans Day. Livestream ceremony 6:00 to 6:45 pm Mountain time on the Boulder Chamber's YouTube channel, with a Joint Service Color Guard. The star area on Flagstaff is closed; view it from Chautauqua, Flagstaff Trail, North Boulder Park, or the other open space spots on the City's list. Lit nightly through the end of the year.

Planning the Evening Around It

If you are organizing a group, this is one of the easiest evenings on the Boulder calendar. You do not need tickets, a shuttle, or a parking strategy for the ceremony itself: put the livestream on the big screen at the house with the fireplace going, watch the Color Guard and the speeches, and when the star comes on, take the short drive down to a viewpoint with everyone still in their coats. Chautauqua is the classic choice, a few minutes west of downtown. Or keep it even simpler: stay in for the livestream with the fire going and the hot tub steaming under a November sky, and drive down to a viewpoint the next clear evening, because the star burns every night through the end of the year.

After November 11 the star becomes part of the season's backdrop. It is glowing every evening through the end of the year, over the Lights of December Parade, over Switch on the Holidays switching the Pearl Street Mall on, over St. Nick on the Bricks, and over every quiet weeknight in between. A group that books the canyon for late November or December gets it whether they plan for it or not.
