Fall Color 2026: The Season Is On

Late September in the foothills: the aspens up high have started, the cottonwoods along the creeks are still green, and the next six weeks will move gold down the mountains toward town. If you are organizing a group for peak season, this is the working guide, checked against the agencies that actually run these places.

This is the 2026 season guide. The evergreen version, with everything that does not change, lives on our fall colors hub page.

Where the color stands right now. No agency posts a percentage or a peak date for the Boulder area, and we will not invent one. What the City of Boulder does say, on its own fall colors page, is that the first and second week of October are generally the best window around Boulder, and that timing varies by elevation. High country first, town last. Watch the city's page and the Forest Service's alerts page as your dates approach.

What You Need to Know for the 2026 Season

Chautauqua and the In-Town Color

City of Boulder Open Space and Mountain Parks

Panorama Point and Chautauqua Meadow look out over the whole city canopy, and the city's fall page says plainly that parking there can be a challenge, which in October is an understatement. The city runs a free Park to Park shuttle with satellite parking to Chautauqua; its service window is summer weekends and holidays, so check the shuttle page before counting on it. Mapleton Avenue, the street Sunshine Canyon Drive becomes on the way into town, is lined with maples, and the corner of 9th and Mapleton holds the largest red maple in the state, per the city. Coot Lake to the north, Sawhill Ponds to the east, and the Homestead Trail above South Boulder Creek for the red cottonwoods and sumacs are all on the city's list this year.

Source: Where to See Fall Colors in and Around Boulder, City of Boulder; shuttle details from Park to Park Shuttle, City of Boulder

Brainard Lake: Timed Entry Is Running

US Forest Service, Arapaho and Roosevelt National Forests

Brainard is the classic high-country color stop from Boulder, and this is the one to plan ahead for. The Forest Service's seasonal page, updated September 16 of this year, says a timed-entry reservation is required to park inside the recreation area, with the main gate open from mid-June through mid-October, closing dates weather dependent. You can skip the reservation by parking at Brainard Gateway Trailhead, no reservation needed, but the lake is two miles up from there. There is a day use fee. Dogs must be on a hand-held leash May 1 through Nov 14. Upper trailheads, including Long Lake and Mitchell Lake, opened for the season in July and also require parking reservations, and the trails past them lead into Indian Peaks Wilderness with its own permit rules. If parking inside is the plan, buy the reservation through recreation.gov; the Forest Service page links it directly.

Source: Brainard Lake Seasonal Information, US Forest Service

Caribou Ranch: Open, With Rules

Boulder County Parks and Open Space

Caribou Ranch, off the Peak to Peak near Nederland, is in its open season; the annual closure runs April 1 through June 30, for the birds and the elk, so fall visitors are clear. The county lists the Blue Bird Loop at 1.8 miles moderate and the DeLode Trail at 1.2 miles easy, aspen along both. It is day use only: no pets, no bikes, no camping, no open fires. Twenty-five parking spots, and when the lot fills, the county allows parking on the south side of County Road 126.

Source: Caribou Ranch, Boulder County; current county restrictions at Boulder County's site

The Drive That Ties It Together

Colorado Department of Transportation

The Peak to Peak Scenic Byway is the spine of a fall trip here: 55 miles, about 80 minutes of driving per CDOT, Colorado's oldest scenic byway, north through Nederland, Ward, and up toward Estes Park. Start it from Sunshine Canyon Drive and you are on a color drive before you even reach the byway proper. CDOT's page points the way to the gravel roads that cross toward lakes, trailheads, and old mining towns if your group wants to wander.

Source: Peak to Peak, Colorado Department of Transportation

Check Before You Go

This is leaf-peeping season, and Mother Nature runs it on her own calendar: the aspens turn gold when they turn, a closure or a condition can change any week, and the best-laid plan bends a little. The agencies post current conditions, and a two-minute check is part of a good morning in the foothills:

Planning the Weekend Around It

The shape of a good fall weekend from Sunshine Canyon Retreat is short: a morning up high before the lots fill, the middle of the day in town on Mapleton or Pearl Street while the sun is warm, and the evening back on the deck watching the light go through the canyon. The house sleeps a group comfortably, sits between the high country and the town color, and has a hot tub for the cold end of an aspen day.
