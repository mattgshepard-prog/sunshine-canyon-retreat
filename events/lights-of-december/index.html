The Lights of December Parade: Boulder's Nighttime Holiday Parade

Each December, downtown Boulder spends one Saturday evening rolling lit-up floats down the middle of its streets. The Lights of December Parade is the city's nighttime send-off to the holiday season: floats built by local businesses, churches, schools, and community groups, marching bands, fire engines strung with lights, carolers, and, at the grand finale, Santa himself. This page is the evergreen version: what the parade is, how the route works, and what a group should plan around, updated each season.

This season's page, with the December 12, 2026 details, is our 2026 Lights of December preview.

What the Parade Is

Downtown Boulder Partnership, boulderdowntown.com

The parade is the flagship night of Light Up the Holidays, downtown Boulder's holiday program of events and activities that runs from late November through December, produced by Downtown Boulder Community Initiatives, a 501c3 organization. The floats are created and decorated by local businesses, churches, schools, community groups, and nonprofits, and the parade brings marching bands, fire engines, and holiday carolers along with it, finishing with an appearance by the jolly old man himself. The organizer caps the field at fifty parade entries, and in 2026 every slot was filled by early October, with a waitlist open behind them. Downtown Boulder does not treat this as a small thing, and neither should you: it is the night the whole season has been building toward.

Source: Lights of December Parade, Downtown Boulder; season context from Light Up the Holidays, Downtown Boulder

The Route, and Where to Watch

The route is a compact loop at the east end of downtown: it starts at 15th and Walnut, heads west to Broadway, turns north on Broadway, turns east on Spruce, and finishes at 15th and Spruce. The whole loop sits a short walk from anywhere on or near Pearl Street, which is why those corners fill up early and hold their spots like claims in a gold rush. The reviewing stand sits at Broadway and Pearl, where each participating group gets announced as it passes, and the organizer points out there are plenty of good spots nearby to grab a warm drink while you wait.

The one rule the organizer repeats: stay behind the fencing and out of the streets for the duration of the parade. If your group includes a stroller or a wheelchair, claim a stretch of fencing early rather than working in from the sidelines after the first horn, because the curb line is the whole show and there is no second row.

Source: Lights of December Parade, Downtown Boulder

Make a Day of It

Parade day stacks up downtown, in the best way. In 2026 the parade fell on Saturday, December 12, and the same day, a short walk up the mall, the Downtown Boulder Visitor Information Center at 1303 Pearl Street held St. Nick on the Bricks, free visits with Santa from 11 am to 2 pm. The weekend before, the COLDERBolder 5K takes over the CU campus, and the whole Light Up the Holidays season, from the Switch on the Holidays night in late November through the parade, is strung along the same few blocks of Pearl Street. A December week in Boulder can hold a race, a Santa visit, and a night parade without ever driving far.

Source: St. Nick on the Bricks, Downtown Boulder; season calendar from Light Up the Holidays, Downtown Boulder

What a Group Should Plan Around

The cold is the real headliner. This parade runs after dark in mid-December, and the cold on a Boulder sidewalk is dry and serious: hats over hair, boots over shoes, hand warmers for the kids, and a thermos if you are the kind of planner who owns one. In 2026 the parade started at 6 pm and the season calendar listed the window as 6 to 7:30 pm, so plan the warm-up for after, not just the wait before.

Walking beats circling. The blocks around the route fill up on parade night, and twelve people move faster on foot than three cars do circling one-way streets in the dark. Park once, walk in together, and let the youngest and oldest members of the group set the pace.

It ends early enough to matter. The parade is short and sweet, which is a mercy when your crew spans four generations. Back up the canyon by eight, the fire is going, and the cocoa happens on the deck under stars instead of under streetlights.

Where to Stay for Parade Night

The lodging question for this parade is the same as for every December weekend in Boulder: one house beats four hotel rooms. Sunshine Canyon Retreat sleeps up to fourteen guests in five bedrooms and four bathrooms across about 4,100 square feet, fifteen minutes from Pearl Street, with a hot tub for the cold walk from the car. The canyon in December is very dark and very quiet, and the town below is lit for the holidays; drive down at dusk and the lights come up as you drop out of the canyon toward the parade.
