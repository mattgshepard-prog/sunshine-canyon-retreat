Boulder Ballet's The Nutcracker: A Thanksgiving Weekend at Macky

Thanksgiving weekend in Boulder has a proper finale this year. Boulder Ballet brings The Nutcracker to Macky Auditorium on Friday, November 27 and Sunday, November 29, 2026, both at 4 pm, with the Boulder Philharmonic Orchestra playing Tchaikovsky live. And new this season there is a second, gentler way in: The Nutcracker Suite, a one-hour version on Sunday at 1 pm built for young audiences and first-time theatergoers. If you are the one holding the whole holiday weekend together for a house full of people, this is the anchor the schedule hangs from.

Every fact below comes from the organizer's own pages, checked October 27, 2026. We will keep this page current as the weekend approaches.

The Facts As They Stand

  • What: Boulder Ballet presents The Nutcracker and The Nutcracker Suite, with live music from the Boulder Philharmonic Orchestra.
  • Where: Macky Auditorium Concert Hall, 1595 Pleasant St, on the CU Boulder campus.
  • The Nutcracker: Friday, November 27 and Sunday, November 29, 2026, both at 4 pm. The full-length production, roughly 90 minutes plus intermission.
  • The Nutcracker Suite: Sunday, November 29 at 1 pm, running about one hour. New this season: a shortened version designed for young audiences and anyone new to ballet, with the orchestra still playing live.
  • For families: The Suite ticket includes meet-the-character photo moments and a First Time Nutcracker Field Guide, a kid-friendly walkthrough of the story and what to watch for.
  • Tickets: Official tickets are sold only through the Macky Auditorium box office at colorado.edu/macky. The organizer says it plainly: tickets from any other seller are invalid.
  • Official page: boulderballet.org/the-nutcracker-2026

All event facts from Boulder Ballet's own Nutcracker 2026 page and Nutcracker ticketing FAQ, checked October 27, 2026.

Two Ways In, and Which One Is Yours

The organizer has essentially split the audience in two, which is a kindness to anyone planning for a mixed-age group. The full The Nutcracker is the complete classic: the party scene, the battle, the snow, the Land of Sweets, Clara's whole dreamworld, roughly 90 minutes plus an intermission. That is the one for a group of adults and teenagers, the people who can sit through a second act and will still be talking about the snow scene in the car.

The Nutcracker Suite is the other door. It is an hour, it is built for first-time theatergoers and young audiences, and it keeps the live orchestra, the dancing, and the theatrical magic in a gentler package. Your ticket includes meet-the-character photo moments and a field guide written for kids. This is the one for the family whose five-year-old has been hearing about the mouse king all month: all of the wonder, home before anyone melts down.

Both productions are performed with the Boulder Philharmonic Orchestra playing live, which is the detail that makes either one worth the drive. A recording is fine at home; a pit full of musicians is why you put on real shoes and go.

Buying Tickets Without Heartbreak

The ticketing rule here is unusually blunt, and worth honoring. Boulder Ballet sells official tickets only through the Macky Auditorium box office at colorado.edu/macky, by phone at 303-492-8008, or in person at the box office at 972 Broadway. The organizer states that resale marketplaces and secondary brokers are not authorized sellers, and that Boulder Ballet and Macky cannot verify or guarantee tickets bought that way. For a family holiday outing, that is a risk with no upside.

If you are deciding late: the only on-site sales happen at the door, in the west lobby box office, which generally opens one hour before show time and sells tickets only for that performance. It is a real option, but not the one to build a fourteen-person plan around.

Ticketing rules from Boulder Ballet's Nutcracker ticketing FAQ, checked October 27, 2026.

Getting to Macky, and Where the Car Sleeps

Macky Auditorium sits on the CU Boulder campus at 1595 Pleasant Street, a few blocks northeast of downtown. The auditorium's own parking page is honest about the hard part: parking around Macky is limited, usually carries a fee, and on popular nights you may end up several blocks away and walking. Build in the extra time.

The page's own recommendations: the Grandview garage and the small Pennsylvania Avenue Lot are both about a five-minute walk away, the Euclid garage about ten minutes, the Folsom garage about twelve. Accessible parking is reserved in Lot 380, east of the hall along Pleasant Street. And the simplest trick of all: ride-share drivers have free access to the front of Macky for drop-off and pickup, which on a cold November evening is worth a lot.

Planning the Weekend Around It

Thanksgiving weekend is the one stretch of the Boulder calendar where the whole town is dressed up at once, and this production lands right in the middle of it. A weekend that holds together well looks something like this: Thursday morning, half the house runs the Thanksgiving Day 5K on Pearl Street while the other half holds down the kitchen. Friday, the feast is history and the 4 pm curtain gives everyone a reason to be showered and out the door by three. Saturday is for the canyon, a long slow morning and whatever the weather allows up high. Sunday at 1 pm, the youngest crowd gets The Nutcracker Suite, with photos with the characters after, and the older crowd can claim the full Friday or Sunday performance as their own.

By then the downtown lights are on for the season, switched on the Sunday before Thanksgiving at Switch on the Holidays, so the drive home from Macky runs through a town that is already glowing.

If your group is staying with us, the house sleeps up to fourteen in five bedrooms, about fifteen minutes up the canyon from Pearl Street, and the run down to Macky is a straight shot: Sunshine Canyon Drive into town, across, and you are at the hall. The evening writes itself: the show, the drive back up the canyon with the heater going and someone humming the sugar plum music, and a hot tub under the stars for everyone who sat still through a whole ballet and deserves it.

If You Are Coming for It

Thanksgiving weekend is one of the busiest lodging weekends of the Boulder year, and the shows that anchor it are the reason. If the plan is the feast Thursday, the ballet Friday, and a lazy Sunday before the drive home, the earlier the house is locked in, the better the whole weekend tends to go.
